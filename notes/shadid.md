Notes on the translation by إسكندر شديد in [الأناجيل المنحولة](https://archive.org/details/20221113_20221113_1619/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%84%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D8%AD%D9%88%D9%84%D8%A9).

The book does not state its source language. These notes collect places where Shadid's Arabic departs from the Coptic in ways that point to a secondary (European) source, or to harmonization with the Arabic Bible.

Comparisons:

- **English:** Thomas O. Lambdin's translation, the standard English rendering of the Coptic (quoted from memory; verify before citing).
- **French:** A. Guillaumont, H.-C. Puech et al., *L'Évangile selon Thomas* (Paris, 1959), as given by the [Bibliothek der Kirchenväter](https://bkv.unifr.ch/fr/works/nhc-22/versions/livre-de-levangile-selon-thomas/divisions) (Université de Fribourg).
- **Doresse:** Jean Doresse, *Les livres secrets des gnostiques d'Égypte*, vol. 2: *L'Évangile selon Thomas, ou les paroles secrètes de Jésus* (Paris, 1959), checked so far only through its English translation, *The Secret Books of the Egyptian Gnostics* (1960; [public upload](https://archive.org/details/the-secret-books-of-the-egyptian-gnostics-jean-doresse-berserker-books-finalfinal)).
  The French is a lending-only book on archive.org ([bwb_S0-BYS-989](https://archive.org/details/bwb_S0-BYS-989)).

## Summary so far

Shadid follows neither Lambdin nor Guillaumont consistently.

- **Agrees with Guillaumont against Lambdin:** 0 «نسخها» (*transcrites*), 1 «لا يذوق الموت» (*ne goûtera pas*), 2أ «ألا يتوقف» (*ne cesse de*), 6هـ «في وجه السماء» (*à la face du ciel*).
- **Agrees with Lambdin against Guillaumont:** 6ج «وسيلة تغذية» ("diet"; Guillaumont *en matière de nourriture*), 6هـ «تكرهونه» ("hate"; Guillaumont *récusez*), 7ب «ملعون» ("cursed"; Guillaumont *souillé*).
- **Matches neither:** 2ج «العالم», 3أ «يستميلونكم», 3أ–ب «فيه», 3د «يعرفونكم», 3هـ «عُري», 4أ «على… ألا يتأخر», 4ب «فسوف يبدو أن».

His likely source is Doresse's French translation (1959): Shadid uses Doresse's numbering exactly (see Numbering below), and several readings that match neither Lambdin nor Guillaumont match Doresse's English:

- 3أ «يستميلونكم»: Doresse "those who seek to attract you".
- 4أ «المثقل بالأيام»: Doresse "heavy with days".
- 4ب «فسوف يبدو أن»: Doresse "it will be seen that".

Other readings still differ from Doresse's English: 3د «يعرفونكم» (Doresse "you will be known"), 4أ «يتأخر» (Doresse "hesitate"), 3هـ «عُري» (Doresse "a state of poverty").
These need checking against the French, which Shadid would have used; the English is itself a translation of it.
Other candidates, if the French does not settle it: Ménard (1975, French), Leloup (1986, French), and the 1959 English edition by Guillaumont et al.

## Numbering

Shadid numbers the sayings 1–118, not 1–114. The 118-saying division is Doresse's, and Shadid's numbers match Doresse's throughout, except at one point (below).
`notes/shadid-index.tsv` maps each standard saying to Shadid's number and to the scan and printed page.

Differences from the standard numbering, all shared with Doresse:

- Standard 1 is unnumbered, printed as part of the prologue, so Shadid 1 is standard 2.
- Split sayings: 3 → 2, 3; 11 → 11, 12; 19 → 20–22; 21 → 24–26.
- Merged sayings: 59 and 60 → 64.

The one difference from Doresse: standard 54 ("Blessed are the poor…") is missing, and Shadid's numbers skip 60.

| Standard | Doresse | Shadid |
|---|---|---|
| 53 | 58 | ٥٨ |
| 54 | 59 | (missing) |
| 55 | 60 | ٥٩ |
| 56 | 61 | ٦١ |

So Shadid (or his typesetter) dropped Doresse's 59 and gave Doresse's 60 the number ٥٩.
From ٦١ on, the numbers agree with Doresse's again.

His introduction says the gospel was written «في سوريا باللغة القبطية» in the second century.
The usual view is that it was composed in Greek (Syria is a common guess for its origin), and the Coptic is a translation.

## Sayings 0–5

### Closer to the Coptic than Lambdin

- **1:** «لا يذوق الموت».
  The Coptic ϥⲛⲁϫⲓϯⲡⲉ ⲁⲛ ⲙ̄ⲡⲙⲟⲩ is literally "will not taste death"; Lambdin has "will not experience death".
  Guillaumont has *ne goûtera pas de la mort*. (It is also the Arabic Bible idiom, John 8:52.)
- **2أ:** «على من يبحث ألا يتوقف عن البحث».
  Keeps the negative of ⲙ̄ⲡⲣ̄ⲧⲣⲉϥⲗⲟ ("let him not cease"), as does Guillaumont (*ne cesse de chercher*).
  Lambdin has the positive "Let him who seeks continue seeking".
- **3أ:** «الذين يستميلونكم» ("those who draw you over").
  Closer to the literal sense of ⲥⲱⲕ ("draw, drag") than Lambdin's "those who lead you" or Guillaumont's *ceux qui vous guident*.
- **3د:** «يعرفونكم» ("they will know you").
  Keeps the third person plural of ⲥⲉⲛⲁⲥⲟⲩⲱⲛⲧⲏⲛⲉ.
  Lambdin ("you will become known") and Guillaumont (*vous serez connus*) both use the passive.
- **5أ:** «قبالة وجهك».
  Keeps ϩⲟ ("face") from ⲙ̄ⲡⲙ̄ⲧⲟ ⲙ̄ⲡⲉⲕϩⲟ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ, as does Guillaumont (*devant ton visage*); Lambdin has "what is in your sight".
  Guillaumont reads ⲡⲉⲧ- as a person (*Celui qui*); Shadid's «ما هو» reads it as a thing.

### Departures from the Coptic

- **0:** «ونسخها» ("copied it") for ⲁϥⲥϩⲁⲓ̈ⲥⲟⲩ ("wrote them").
  Matches Guillaumont's *qu'a transcrites*; Lambdin has "wrote down".
- **2ج:** «ويملك العالم» ("will rule the world").
  ⲉϫⲙ̄ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ is "over the All", a technical term; Lambdin has "over the all", Guillaumont *sur le Tout*.
- **3أ, 3ب:** «تكون طيور السماء **فيه** قبلكم», «تكون الأسماك **فيه** قبلكم».
  فيه ("in it / there") is not in the Coptic ⲛⲁⲣϣⲟⲣⲡ ⲉⲣⲱⲧⲛ̄ ("will precede you"), nor in Lambdin or Guillaumont (*vous devanceront*).
- **3هـ:** «في عُري… وأنتم [مَنْ تكونون] العُري» ("nakedness").
  ⲙⲛ̄ⲧϩⲏⲕⲉ is "poverty"; Lambdin has "poverty", Guillaumont *pauvreté*.
  The source of «عُري» is still unexplained.
  The bracketed «[مَنْ تكونون]» is a translator's supplement, not in the Coptic.
- **4أ:** «على الشيخ… ألا يتأخر» ("the old man must not delay").
  The Coptic ϥⲛⲁϫⲛⲁⲩ ⲁⲛ is a future statement ("will not hesitate"), as in Lambdin and Guillaumont (*n'hésitera pas*), not an obligation.
  «الوليد» omits ⲕⲟⲩⲉⲓ ("small"; Guillaumont *un tout petit enfant*).
- **4ب:** «فسوف يبدو أن» ("for it will appear that") is not in the Coptic, which has only ϫⲉ ("for"), nor in Guillaumont (*parce que*).
  «واحداً [أحداً]» gives a bracketed alternative for ⲟⲩⲁ ⲟⲩⲱⲧ.
- **5ج:** «فما من شيء خفي إلا وينكشف»: the same «ما من… إلا و…» construction he uses in 6و.

## Saying 6

### Evidence for a European source

- **6ج:** «وأي وسيلة تغذية نتقيد بها» ("what means of nourishment do we abide by").
  The Coptic ⲉⲛⲛⲁⲣ̄ⲡⲁⲣⲁⲧⲏⲣⲉⲓ ⲉⲟⲩ ⲛ̄ϭⲓⲟⲩⲱⲙ has only ⲟⲩⲱⲙ, "eating/food".
  "Diet" is an interpretation, and matches Lambdin's "What *diet* should we *observe*?".
  Guillaumont is closer to the Coptic: *qu'observerons-nous en matière de nourriture?*
  Someone reading the Coptic directly would more likely write طعام or أكل.
- **6هـ:** «فكل تلك الأمور ظاهرة في وجه السماء».
  The Coptic ⲥⲉϭⲟⲗⲡ⳿ ⲧⲏⲣⲟⲩ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲙ̄ⲡⲉⲙⲧⲟ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲛ̄ⲧⲡⲉ means "they are all uncovered before heaven".
  ظاهرة matches Lambdin's "all things are *plain*", not ϭⲟⲗⲡ "uncovered" (Guillaumont *dévoilé*).
  في وجه السماء is a calque of Guillaumont's *à la face du ciel*; ⲙ̄ⲡⲉⲙⲧⲟ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ is more naturally أمام.
  «تكرهونه» matches Lambdin's "hate", against Guillaumont's *récusez*.
- **6أ:** «تريدنا أن نصوم؟» is a question, as in Lambdin and Guillaumont (*Veux-tu que nous jeûnions?*).
  The Coptic ϫⲉⲕ̂ⲟⲩⲱϣ ⲉⲧⲣⲛ̄︤ⲣ︥ⲛⲏⲥⲧⲉⲩⲉ is an unmarked Present I.
  Weak evidence on its own, since Coptic questions can be unmarked.

### Harmonization with the Arabic Bible

- **6و–6ز:** «ما من شيء خفي إلا وينكشف وما من شيء مستور إلا ويُعلن».
  The Coptic ending ⲉⲩⲛⲁϭⲱ ⲟⲩⲉϣⲛ̄ ϭⲟⲗⲡϥ⳿ means "will remain without uncovering it".
  Lambdin has "without being *uncovered*" and Guillaumont *sans être dévoilé*; both would give يُكشف.
  يُعلن more likely echoes Van Dyck's Mark 4:22 «إِلَّا لِيُعْلَنَ».

### Omissions

- **6و:** ⲅⲁⲣ ("for") is not translated.
- **6ز:** ⲉⲩⲛⲁϭⲱ ("will remain") is not translated; the «ما من… إلا و…» construction replaces it.

## Saying 7

The archive.org OCR text drops the first line of this saying (in the middle of printed page 175, not at a page break).
The full text, read from the page image (scan index 176):
«٧. قال يسوع: «طوبى لذلك الأسد الذي يأكله الإنسان بحيث يصبح الأسد إنساناً. إنما ملعون الإنسان الذي يأكله الأسد بحيث يصبح الأسد انساناً!»

### Evidence for a European source

- **7ب:** «ملعون» ("cursed").
  The Coptic ϥⲃⲏⲧ is the stative of ⲃⲱⲧⲉ, "be polluted, abominable"; Guillaumont has *souillé* ("defiled").
  "Cursed" is Lambdin's rendering ("and cursed is the man whom the lion consumes").
- **7أ–7ب:** «طوبى لذلك الأسد الذي يأكله الإنسان بحيث يصبح الأسد إنساناً» joins the first two clauses as "blessed is that lion which man eats, so that the lion becomes man".
  Lambdin restructures this way ("Blessed is the lion which becomes man when consumed by man"), and Guillaumont's *Heureux est le lion que l'homme mangera, et le lion sera homme* also runs them together.
  The Coptic keeps them separate: 7أ ends at ⲛⲁⲟⲩⲟⲙϥ, and ⲁⲩⲱ ⲛ̄ⲧⲉⲡⲙⲟⲩⲉⲓ ϣⲱⲡⲉ ⲣ̄ⲣⲱⲙⲉ begins a new coordinated clause.

### Closer to the Coptic

- **7أ (weak):** «لذلك الأسد» ("that lion") may reflect the demonstrative ⲡⲁⲉⲓ in ⲡⲙⲟⲩⲉⲓ ⲡⲁⲉⲓ ⲉⲧⲉ‑, which neither Lambdin ("the lion which") nor Guillaumont (*le lion que*) keeps.
  But ⲡⲁⲉⲓ ⲉⲧⲉ‑ is the ordinary Coptic way of saying "the one which", so «ذلك» may just be Shadid's style.

### Interpretive additions

- **7ب:** «إنما» adds a contrast; the Coptic has only ⲁⲩⲱ ("and").
- **7أ, 7ج:** «بحيث» ("so that") turns ⲁⲩⲱ ("and") into a result clause in both halves, making them parallel.
- **7أ, 7ب:** «يأكله» is present in both halves; the Coptic ⲛⲁⲟⲩⲟⲙϥ is future (Guillaumont *mangera*).
- A footnote reads the saying as matter (the lion) against spirit (the divine element in man).

### Textual reading

- **7ج:** Shadid keeps the manuscript reading "the lion becomes man", not the common emendation "the man becomes lion".
  Lambdin and Guillaumont also keep the manuscript reading, so this does not distinguish between sources.
